- "服务端没 id36 代理" is not accurate: `GET /l/:id36` **is** the server-side proxy; a

## Multiplexing findings

Transport-level multiplexing already exists: the browser talks HTTP/2 to Cloudflare, and
the SharedWorker's image pool (`imagePoolSize` from hardwareConcurrency/downlink) runs
several transcode+upload jobs concurrently over that one connection. Request count per
upload is already minimal: exactly one `POST /upload` per artifact, no HEAD preflight, and
the CORS preflight is cached for a day (`Access-Control-Max-Age`). Batching multiple
artifacts into one multipart request would save requests only at the cost of per-file
progress, retry granularity, and the facade contract — not worth it unless real quota
pain shows up. View-side requests are already absorbed: `/l/:id36` answers
`Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`.

## Upload failures: two deadlines, one gate

Operator report: multi-file drops fail easily, and large files "stick at the very end"
rather than failing outright. Both reproduced from the code, no server involved.

- **The tail of a large artifact was measured with the body's silence budget.**
  `UPLOAD_TIMEOUT_MS = 45s` restarts only on `xhr.upload.onprogress`, which stops firing the
  moment the body is out; from there the whole server-side tail (facade relays the artifact
  upstream, upstream stores it, response returns) sat on that same clock. A response arriving
  later than 45s was aborted as `timeout` — `uploadClient.test.ts`'s `late` case asserted
  exactly that. The visible half: the card curtain tops out at `CURTAIN_MAX_OPEN = 0.9`, so an
  upload at 100% still shows a veil across the top edge, which is what "stuck at the last bit"
  actually was. Fix: the body's end switches the attempt to `UPLOAD_RESPONSE_TIMEOUT_MS = 120s`.
- **The upload leg had no ceiling of its own; it inherited the image pool.** `imagePoolSize`
  is sized for cores (up to 6) and bounds transcoding, but a job holds its slot through the
  upload too, so one multi-file drop opened up to six 100 MB POSTs at once against a single
  upstream, plus up to two video uploads. A failure is terminal (`runUpload` attempts once,
  `failJob` drops the resume record), and the `retry-after` / `x-ratelimit-*` headers the
  facade deliberately exposes are never read by the client — so a transient rate-limit or one
  stalled stream permanently failed that file. Fix: one gate in `sw.ts`
  (`uploadPoolSize()` = 3, or 2 below a 2 Mbps downlink) that every upload leg passes through.

Not fixed, and left for the operator to weigh: `transcodeImage` decodes and re-encodes at full
resolution with no downscale, and the pool runs up to six of those on the one SharedWorker
thread — roughly 2 × w × h × 4 bytes per job, so ~190 MB for a 24 MP photo. `MAX_IMAGE_DECODE_BYTES`
bounds the _file_, not the decoded pixels. A worker OOM takes every in-flight job down with it
(`restartWithBackoff` fails all snapshots as `worker_died`), so this is the other candidate for
"concurrency fails easily".
